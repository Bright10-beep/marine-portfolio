Featured Projects

Seabed Mapping in the Gulf of Guinea

🧭 Technical Explanation: Seabed Mapping in the Gulf of Guinea involves the systematic survey and analysis of the ocean floor using advanced geophysical tools such as multibeam echosounders, side-scan sonar, and sub-bottom profilers. This process generates high-resolution bathymetric data that reveals the morphology, sediment composition, and structural features of the seafloor. Located along the western coast of Africa, the Gulf of Guinea is a region of significant geological and economic interest. Seabed mapping in this area supports: Resource exploration (hydrocarbons, marine minerals) Tectonic and sedimentological studies Habitat mapping for marine biodiversity Pipeline and cable route planning Navigation safety and coastal development Through this work, marine geologists gain deeper insight into the tectonic framework and depositional history of the region, aiding both academic research and industrial applications.

Coastal Erosion Analysis

Coastal Erosion Analysis involves the assessment and monitoring of shoreline retreat and sediment displacement caused by hydrodynamic forces such as wave action, tidal currents, storm surges, and sea-level rise. The process typically includes the use of remote sensing, historical shoreline data, GIS modeling, and field-based measurements to quantify erosion rates and identify vulnerable zones. This analysis plays a critical role in: Coastal zone management and planning Designing protective structures (e.g., sea walls, groynes) Understanding sediment transport dynamics Assessing the impact of climate change on coastal systems By understanding erosion patterns, marine geologists and coastal engineers can develop strategies to mitigate loss of land and infrastructure in coastal regions.
